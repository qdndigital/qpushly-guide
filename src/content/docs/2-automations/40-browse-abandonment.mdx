**Fires when:** a subscriber viewed a product and did not buy it.

Most shoppers never reach the cart. Browse abandonment is the follow-up for the
much larger group who looked, thought about it, and left.

## How it differs from cart recovery

| | Abandoned cart | Browse abandonment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | Cart went quiet | Product viewed, not bought |
| Intent | High — they had committed | Lower — they were considering |
| Right tone | "Your cart is waiting" | "Here's what you were looking at" |
| Volume | Lower | Much higher |

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not reuse your cart copy here">
"You left something in your cart!" is wrong and slightly unsettling when the
shopper never added anything. It reads as if you watched them. Write for someone
who was browsing, because that is what happened.
</Callout>

## Set it up

**Automations → Browse Abandonment.**

Start with **one** reminder. This automation has the highest volume of the five —
every product page view by a subscriber is a candidate — so it is also the one
most likely to annoy people if you send three.

Use a longer delay than you would for a cart. A few hours, or the next day, reads
as helpful; twenty minutes reads as surveillance.

## What to write

Reference the product without implying commitment, and give an easy way to look
at alternatives.

> **Title:** Still looking at the Kiln Mug?
> **Message:** It's in Slate and Bone, and both are in stock right now.
> **Primary button:** View it again → the product URL
> **Secondary button:** See the collection → the collection URL

The secondary button matters more here than anywhere else. If they did not buy,
there is a decent chance the reason was the product itself — so offer the
neighbours.

## What it skips

- Products the shopper has since **added to their cart** — abandoned cart takes
  over from there, and the two never both fire for the same item.
- Products they **bought**.

## Volume control

There is no per-day cap in the app. If you find this automation sending more than
you are comfortable with, your levers are:

1. **One reminder only.**
2. **A longer delay** — pushing it to the next day naturally reduces how many
   shoppers still qualify.
3. **Switch it off** and rely on cart recovery plus back-in-stock, which are the
   two automations shoppers rarely object to.

<Callout type="tip">
Watch the click rate on the automations list. If browse abandonment has high
impressions and a click rate well below your other automations, it is being
ignored — which means it is costing you goodwill for nothing. Turn it off.
</Callout>
