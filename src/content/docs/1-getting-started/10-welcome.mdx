Qpushly adds **web push notifications** to your Shopify store. A visitor taps
"Allow" once in their browser, and from then on you can send them a notification
that appears on their desktop or phone — even when they are not on your site.

No email address. No phone number. Nothing for the shopper to type.

## What you can send

**Campaigns** — a message you write and send yourself, now or on a schedule. A
new drop, a sale, a restock announcement.

**Automations** — messages that fire on their own when something happens in your
store. There are five:

| Automation | Sends when |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome notifications | someone subscribes |
| Abandoned cart recovery | a cart sits untouched for ten minutes |
| Browse abandonment | a product was viewed but not bought |
| Back in stock | a variant someone looked at is available again |
| Price drop | a product falls below the price a shopper saw |

Each automation can send up to **three** messages in sequence, on delays you set.

## What web push is not

<Callout type="warning" title="Push is not email">
A push notification is a short interruption on someone's screen. It has a title,
two lines of text, an image and up to two buttons — that is the whole format.
There is no inbox for it to sit in, no way for the shopper to scroll back and
find it later, and no way to send a long message. Treat every send as something
that has to earn the interruption.
</Callout>

Two more limits worth knowing before you plan anything around push:

- **A subscriber is a browser, not a person.** If someone subscribes on their
  laptop and again on their phone, that is two subscribers. If they clear their
  browser data, the subscription is gone and they will need to opt in again.
- **You cannot email a push subscriber.** Qpushly only knows the address their
  browser gave it. Email addresses appear in your subscriber list only where your
  own Shopify order data supplies one.

## Where it runs

Qpushly is an **embedded Shopify app** — it lives inside your Shopify admin, in
the sidebar with everything else. There is no separate dashboard to log into and
no separate account to manage.

The storefront half of it is a **theme app embed**, which you switch on from the
theme editor. That step is not optional, and it is where most setups stall — so
it has [its own page](/docs/getting-started/theme-embed).

## Which browsers can receive notifications

| Platform | Support |
| --- | --- |
| Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera — desktop | Yes |
| Chrome, Firefox, Samsung Internet — Android | Yes |
| Safari — macOS | Yes |
| Safari — iPhone / iPad | Only if the shopper adds your store to their home screen |

<Callout type="note">
Apple requires an installed web app before it will deliver web push on iOS. Most
of your iPhone traffic will therefore never see the prompt. This is a platform
rule, not a Qpushly limitation — every web push tool has it. Your
[subscriber list](/docs/subscribers/subscriber-list) shows the real device mix
for your store, which is more useful than any general statistic.
</Callout>
