Qpushly's storefront half is a **theme app embed**. It is off by default, because
Shopify does not let an app add code to your theme without you switching it on.

<Callout type="danger" title="Nothing works until this is done">
No embed means no service worker on your storefront, which means no visitor can
subscribe, which means every screen in Qpushly stays empty and no automation can
ever fire. If you only do one thing from this guide, do this.
</Callout>

## Steps

1. In your Shopify admin, go to **Online Store → Themes**.
2. On your **live** theme, click **Customize**.
3. In the left sidebar of the theme editor, click the **App embeds** icon (the
   puzzle-piece near the bottom).
4. Find **Qpushly Embed** in the list and switch its toggle **on**.
5. Click **Save**.

## Check that it worked

Open your storefront in a **normal** browser window — not private/incognito, and
not the theme editor's preview.

After a few seconds you should see **Qpushly's invite** (not the browser's own
dialog — that only appears after you click accept). Click the accept button, then
allow notifications when the browser asks.

Then go back to the app: **Qpushly → Subscribers**. Your own browser should now be
in the list, with its browser, OS and device filled in.

<Callout type="tip" title="If you have already dismissed it">
Two separate things remember your answer:

- **The browser**, if you blocked notifications. Click the padlock or tune icon in
  the address bar → **Notifications** → reset to "Ask" (Chrome/Edge: Site settings
  → Notifications → Reset permission).
- **Qpushly's invite**, if you clicked "Not now" — remembered for 14 days. Clear
  the site's local storage, or delete the `qpushly_prompt_dismissed_at` key.

Then reload.
</Callout>

## Things that stop the prompt appearing

**You are on a `.myshopify.com` preview or the theme editor.** Service workers
need a real page load on your storefront domain. Visit the actual storefront URL.

**Your store has password protection on.** A password-protected storefront can
still load the embed, but if you are stuck, turn the password off temporarily
while you test.

**You are testing on iPhone or iPad.** Safari on iOS only delivers web push to a
site the shopper has added to their home screen. Test on desktop Chrome first.

**You already blocked notifications, or declined the invite.** See the tip above —
these are two different pieces of state.

**You enabled the embed on a theme that is not live.** Check you clicked
**Customize** on the theme marked *Current theme*.

## Settings on the embed

Click **Qpushly Embed** in the App embeds list to open its settings.

### Subscribe prompt

Qpushly does **not** go straight to the browser's permission dialog. It shows its
own invite first, and only opens the browser dialog when a shopper accepts.

<Callout type="note" title="Why it works that way">
A browser gives you exactly one permission request per visitor, and a shopper who
blocks notifications can never be asked again — by any app. Spending that one
request on someone who is not interested loses them permanently. Asking with your
own invite first means you only spend it on people who already said yes.

It also matters on Safari: macOS and iOS refuse a permission request that did not
come from a click, so the browser-first approach silently fails there.
</Callout>

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Show the subscribe invite automatically** | Off = Qpushly never asks on its own; you ask from your own button (below). |
| **Wait before showing** | Seconds after page load. A few seconds converts better than asking on arrival. |
| **Position** | Bottom right/left, bottom centre, or top centre. |
| **Invite heading / message** | Your copy. Say what they will actually get. |
| **Accept / Decline button** | Your labels. |

A shopper who declines is not asked again for **14 days**.

### Automation tracking

Two toggles, both on by default:

- **Track carts** — required for [Abandoned cart recovery](/docs/automations/abandoned-cart).
- **Track product views** — required for [Browse abandonment](/docs/automations/browse-abandonment), [Back in stock](/docs/automations/back-in-stock) and [Price drop](/docs/automations/price-drop).

Turning one off stops that script loading on your storefront — useful if you do not
use those automations and want the lightest possible page.

<Callout type="warning">
Turning a tracker off does not disable the automation; it removes the data the
automation needs, so it simply stops finding anyone to send to.
</Callout>

## Asking from your own button

The invite is convenient, but the highest-converting place to ask is a moment when
the shopper obviously wants it — a sold-out size, for instance. Add the attribute
`data-qpushly-subscribe` to any element in your theme:

```html
<button type="button" data-qpushly-subscribe>
  Notify me when it's back
</button>
```

Clicking that element asks for permission and subscribes the visitor. It works
whether or not the automatic invite is switched on.

<Callout type="tip" title="Asking at the right moment gets more subscribers">
An invite that arrives the instant someone lands on your homepage is easy to
dismiss. One that arrives when they have just tried to buy a sold-out size is one
they actually want. If you only add one button, put it on your sold-out product
pages.
</Callout>

## Removing it

Switch the same toggle off and Save. The embed stops loading immediately and
nothing is left behind in your theme. Existing subscribers stay in your list but
stop growing.
