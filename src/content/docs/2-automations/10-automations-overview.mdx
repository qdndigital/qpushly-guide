All five automations work the same way. Learn this page and the five that follow
are just the triggers.

## Automations, reminders, runs

Three words that mean different things in Qpushly:

**Automation** — the type. There are exactly five and you cannot create more.

**Reminder** — a message inside an automation. Up to **three** per automation,
each with its own title, message, buttons, delay and on/off state. Reminder 1
fires first, then 2, then 3.

**Run** — one reminder queued for one subscriber. A run is scheduled, then sent,
and then possibly clicked and converted. Runs are what produce the numbers.

## Delays

Every reminder has a **Delay Time**, measured from the moment the automation's
trigger fired — not from the previous reminder.

So on abandoned cart, delays of 30 minutes, 4 hours and 24 hours mean the shopper
hears from you at 30 minutes, 4 hours and 24 hours after the cart went quiet.

<Callout type="note" title="The editor enforces order">
You cannot set a later reminder to a delay shorter than an earlier one — the
editor disables those options. This prevents reminder 3 arriving before reminder
2, which would read as nonsense to the shopper.
</Callout>

## The reminder limit

The ceiling depends on your plan: **one** reminder per automation on the free plan,
**three** on the paid plan. When you hit it, the **Create Reminder** button is
disabled and shows the count.

Three is the ceiling on any plan.

This is deliberate. Push is an interruption on someone's screen; a fourth message
about the same cart is where merchants start losing subscribers who block
notifications for the domain entirely — and once a shopper blocks you, you cannot
win them back from inside the app.

<Callout type="tip" title="Start with one">
Write one reminder, let it run for a week, look at its clicks, then decide whether
a second earns its place. Most stores find reminder 1 does the bulk of the work.
</Callout>

## Writing a reminder

**Notification Title** — around 40 characters before most browsers truncate. Lead
with the thing, not the greeting.

**Message** — one or two lines. Say what and why. If there is a specific product
involved, name it.

**Primary button** — text plus link. Optional, but a button lifts clicks. Send it
somewhere specific: the cart, the product, the collection — never the homepage.

**Secondary button** — a genuine second option ("See similar", "Not now"), not a
duplicate of the first.

**Status** — active or inactive. Inactive keeps your copy but stops new sends.

## Turning one off

Set its status to inactive. Runs already scheduled for that reminder are not sent.
Nothing is deleted, so you can switch it back on with the copy intact.

## Reading the automations list

The **Automations** page shows all five as cards, each with four numbers:

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Clicks** | Notifications from this automation that were clicked |
| **Impressions** | Notifications delivered |
| **Revenue** | Orders attributed back to this automation |
| **Conv. Rate** | Clicks ÷ impressions, as a percentage |

<Callout type="warning" title="&quot;Conv. rate&quot; here means click rate">
On the automations list, the conversion-rate column is clicks divided by
impressions — that is a *click-through* rate, not an order rate. For orders per
send, compare the Revenue column against Impressions yourself.
</Callout>

## Which automations your plan includes

**Welcome notifications** are available on every plan — including the free one,
because an automation you can actually send is the only way to find out whether
push is worth paying for.

The other four (**abandoned cart**, **browse abandonment**, **back in stock**,
**price drop**) need the paid plan. They are marked with an *Upgrade* badge on the
Automations page. See [Plan and billing](/docs/settings/plan-and-billing).

## Which ones to turn on first

1. **[Welcome notifications](/docs/automations/welcome-notifications)** — the
   easiest win. The subscriber is on your site right now.
2. **[Back in stock](/docs/automations/back-in-stock)** — needs almost no
   copywriting and goes to people who already told you they want the product.
3. **[Abandoned cart](/docs/automations/abandoned-cart)** — the highest value, and
   the one worth spending time on the copy for.
4. **[Browse abandonment](/docs/automations/browse-abandonment)** and
   **[price drop](/docs/automations/price-drop)** — add once you have enough
   subscribers for the numbers to mean anything.

## Testing without waiting

Automations run on a sweep every ten minutes, so nothing is instant. To test:

1. Subscribe yourself from your own storefront.
2. Trigger the event — subscribe for welcome, leave a cart for abandoned cart,
   edit a product's stock or price for the other two.
3. Wait for the delay plus up to ten minutes.

If nothing arrives, work through
[Troubleshooting](/docs/help/troubleshooting).
