## The numbers on a campaign

**Impressions** — notifications delivered to a browser. Reach, not attention.

**Clicks** — the shopper tapped the notification or one of its buttons.

**Orders** — orders Qpushly was able to attribute to this campaign.

**Revenue** — the value of those orders.

**CTR** — clicks ÷ impressions.

The Campaigns list also shows totals across all campaigns, and each row carries a
status: `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `PUBLISHED` or `ARCHIVED`.

## How attribution actually works

Worth understanding, because it explains both the numbers you get and the ones you
don't:

1. A shopper **clicks** the notification. The link carries a campaign tag.
2. The embed stores that tag and **writes it onto the shopper's cart** as a cart
   attribute.
3. The shopper checks out.
4. Shopify sends Qpushly the **order**, carrying the cart attribute.
5. Qpushly matches the tag and credits the order to the campaign.

## Where attribution misses

Every attribution model has holes. These are Qpushly's, so you know which way the
numbers lean:

**The click window expires.** The campaign tag on a click is valid for about
**30 minutes**. A shopper who taps a notification at lunchtime and buys that
evening is not attributed — the order is real, the credit is not.

**Cross-device buying.** They tap on their phone, buy on their laptop. The tag was
on the phone's cart. Unattributed.

**Clearing the cart.** Some themes and apps rebuild the cart in a way that drops
custom attributes. The tag goes with them.

**No click, but influenced.** A shopper sees the notification, does not tap it,
opens your site directly and buys. Nothing to attribute.

<Callout type="warning" title="Attributed revenue is a floor, not a total">
Every one of the gaps above pushes the reported number *down*, never up. So treat
attributed revenue as the minimum push earned you. It is useful for comparing
campaigns against each other — the bias is consistent — but it will understate
push's real contribution.
</Callout>

## Reading the numbers usefully

**Compare like with like.** Campaign A against campaign B, both measured the same
way, is a fair comparison. Campaign revenue against your email platform's revenue
is not — the two attribute completely differently, and both will claim the same
order.

**Watch CTR, not impressions.** Impressions grow with your list whatever you write.
CTR tells you whether the writing worked.

**Look for a falling CTR across consecutive sends.** That is subscriber fatigue,
and it arrives before the unsubscribes you cannot see.

**A high CTR with no orders** means the notification promised something the landing
page did not deliver. Check the link.

## What is not reported

- **Unsubscribes.** When a shopper blocks notifications, their browser does not
  tell your store. There is no unsubscribe count anywhere in the app, and
  subscriber count therefore overstates your reachable audience.
- **Dismissals.** No way to know a notification was swiped away unread.
- **Per-day breakdowns.** Campaign counters are running totals.
- **Revenue net of refunds.** Attributed revenue is order value at the time of the
  order.
