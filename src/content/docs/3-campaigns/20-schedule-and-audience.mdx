## Send now or schedule

**Publish time** offers an immediate send or a scheduled one. Scheduling is the
better default for anything that is not urgent — you can write it when you have
time and let it land when your shoppers are awake.

<Callout type="warning" title="Scheduled sends land in a ten-minute window">
Qpushly processes scheduled work on a sweep that runs every ten minutes. A campaign
set for 09:00 goes out between 09:00 and 09:10.

For a drop announcement or a sale, that is irrelevant. For anything where the
notification has to arrive *before* a specific moment — a countdown, a livestream,
a doors-open time — schedule it **fifteen minutes early** and let it be early
rather than late.
</Callout>

## Picking a time

Push arrives on someone's phone or desktop the moment it is delivered. There is no
inbox to wait in, so timing matters more than it does for email.

- **Avoid night hours in your main market.** A notification at 3am is how you get
  blocked. Qpushly has no quiet-hours setting, so this is entirely down to when you
  schedule.
- **Your subscribers' timezones are recorded** — the subscriber list shows the
  spread. Send for wherever most of them are, not for wherever you are.
- **Mid-morning and early evening** are the conventional windows. Test against your
  own click numbers rather than trusting that.

## End date

Optional. Use it for a campaign that stops being true — a weekend sale, a
limited-quantity drop. It stops the campaign continuing to count as live after the
offer has gone.

## Target audience

Chooses who receives the campaign. The default sends to your whole subscriber list,
which is the right choice for most announcements.

Reasons to narrow it:

- The message only applies to some devices (an Android-specific issue, say).
- You are testing copy and want a smaller group first.

<Callout type="note" title="Audience is not behavioural segmentation">
Qpushly segments subscribers by browser, operating system and device — attributes
of the subscription. It does not build segments from purchase history, spend, or
tags. If you need "everyone who bought in the last 90 days", that is not something
this app can target.
</Callout>

## Campaign type

**Regular** — the default; use it for anything ordinary.

**Flash** — for short-lived, time-boxed sends. Pair it with an end date so the
campaign closes itself.

## A test-first habit

Before a big send:

1. Set the audience to something small, or send to yourself first.
2. Check the notification on a **phone** as well as a desktop — truncation differs.
3. Tap the button and confirm the landing page is right.
4. Then send to everyone.

The cost of a broken link in a push notification is higher than in an email: there
is no way to follow up with a correction that the shopper will connect to the
original.
