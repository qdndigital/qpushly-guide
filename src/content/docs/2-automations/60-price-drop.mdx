**Fires when:** a product falls below the price a subscriber saw when they viewed it.

For shoppers whose hesitation was the price, this is the message that resolves it.

## How it works

When a subscriber views a product, Qpushly stores the price at that moment.
Shopify tells the app when the product changes; if the new price is lower than the
price that shopper saw, they get the alert.

The comparison is **per shopper**, against what *they* saw — not against your
compare-at price. Two people who viewed the same product at different prices can
therefore get different treatment, which is the correct behaviour.

<Callout type="note">
A price increase never sends anything.
</Callout>

## Set it up

**Automations → Price Drop.**

One reminder, short delay. Include both prices — the drop is the whole message, and
a number they recognise makes it credible.

> **Title:** Price drop on something you viewed
> **Message:** Harbour Canvas Tote is now £38, down from £52.
> **Button:** See the deal → the product URL

## When not to use this

Price-drop alerts are the automation most likely to work against you. Two cases:

**You discount often.** If shoppers learn that waiting produces a notification
with a lower number, you have trained them to wait. Full-price sales fall. If your
calendar has a sale every month, leave this one off.

**You are about to run a site-wide sale.** A 30%-off weekend can fire a price-drop
alert for every product every subscriber has ever viewed. There is no built-in cap
on that.

<Callout type="danger" title="Turn this off before a site-wide sale">
Set the reminder to inactive before you apply sale pricing across your catalogue,
and switch it back on afterwards. Otherwise your most engaged subscribers get a
burst of notifications in one afternoon, which is exactly how a shopper decides to
block notifications for your domain — permanently, and with no way for you to
undo it.
</Callout>

## Where it does work well

- **End-of-season markdowns on a handful of lines.** Genuine, infrequent, and the
  people who viewed those items at full price are the right audience.
- **Stores that rarely discount.** Here a price drop is real news.
- **Single-item promotions** — one product, one alert, no blast.

## Things to know

- **It needs the products permission**, granted at install.
- The comparison uses the price recorded at view time, so a shopper who viewed the
  product several times is compared against the most recent price they saw.
- Like every automation, delivery waits for the ten-minute sweep.
