A campaign is a notification you write and send yourself — a drop, a sale, an
announcement. Automations react to events; campaigns are the ones you decide.

## The fields

**Campaign title** — this is both the internal name and the notification's title.
Most browsers show around 40 characters before truncating, so front-load the point.

**Description** — the notification body. One or two lines. Everything past about
120 characters is likely to be cut on a phone.

**Campaign URL** — where a click lands. Use a specific collection or product URL,
never your homepage: a shopper who clicked a notification about one thing and gets
dropped on your front page usually leaves.

**Image** — optional. Uploaded to your own Shopify Files. A wide product shot works
here; a logo does not, because the small square icon already carries your brand.

**Target audience** — who receives it. See
[Scheduling and audience](/docs/campaigns/schedule-and-audience).

**Campaign type** — *Flash* or *Regular*. Flash is for short-lived,
time-boxed sends; regular is the default for everything else.

**Publish time** — send now, or schedule it.

**End date** — optional, for time-limited campaigns.

## What the shopper actually sees

Not everything you fill in. A notification is:

- your small square **icon** (from Settings → default notification icon)
- the **title**
- one or two lines of **body text**
- optionally your **image**
- up to two **buttons**

There is no formatting, no links inside the text, and no way for the shopper to
find the message again once it is dismissed. Write accordingly.

## Writing one that gets clicked

**Lead with the thing.** "Autumn drop is live" beats "We have some news".

**Be specific.** "14 new pieces" beats "lots of new arrivals". A number the shopper
can picture is worth more than an adjective.

**Give one reason to tap now.** Early access, limited quantity, a real deadline —
something true.

**Match the link to the promise.** If the notification says autumn drop, the link
goes to the autumn collection.

<Callout type="tip" title="Read it as an interruption">
Before sending, imagine it appearing over whatever you were doing five minutes
ago. If you would not have tapped it, rewrite it. This one test filters out most
weak sends.
</Callout>

## Frequency

There is no send cap in the app, so this is on you. Push is more intrusive than
email and the punishment for over-sending is worse: a blocked shopper is gone for
good, and nothing in the app can win them back.

A reasonable ceiling for most stores is **two to four campaigns a month**, on top
of whatever the automations send. If in doubt, send less.

## Before you hit send

- The **URL works** and goes where the copy promised.
- The **title reads correctly truncated** at ~40 characters.
- Your **default notification icon** is set in Settings (otherwise browsers show a
  generic bell).
- You are **not about to duplicate an automation** — do not campaign about a
  restock that back-in-stock already covers.
